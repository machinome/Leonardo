One turn / A measured rise

Follow the teeth.

  1. Bottom · phase 0°

    The rack rests at the lower demonstration position. Its hook carries the weight through a separate lifting eye.

  2. Mid-lift · phase 90°

    The crank has turned 68.75°. The 30 mm pitch radius converts that turn into 36 mm of vertical travel.

  3. Top · phase 180°

    The load has risen 72 mm. The crank reverses smoothly and lowers it again. The guides keep the finite rack engaged.

Choose Run and play the timeline for the complete lift and return. Stage buttons hold named poses. Phase describes the up/down cycle, not the crank angle. Intermediate run settings blend that cycle with the selected static pose, reducing its excursion. Every setting loops continuously.

A study, not a load-rated tool

These dimensions and modern involute teeth are Astra's original educational choices. Positive backlash leaves space for the teeth to reverse. Holding the load requires torque at the crank: no brake, lifting capacity, friction or dynamics is simulated. The timber frame is a grounded fixture; joinery is simplified.

The Museo Leonardiano record links this crank, rack and hook to Leonardo's Codex Atlanticus, f.998r. Its 1983 reconstruction is a historical reference, not the geometry shown here. No invention priority is claimed.
