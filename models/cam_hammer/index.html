One rotation / One stroke

Follow the contact.

  1. Lift · 120°

    The cam pushes the short tail down. The lever turns in its journals, raising the hammer on the long arm.

  2. Release · 250°

    The cam retreats faster than the rounded shoe returns. A gap opens: the cam has let the hammer go.

  3. Strike · 320°

    The hammer reaches the anvil's flat face, then rests until the next rise. No material is deformed.

Choose Run and play the viewer's timeline for a full cycle. Stage buttons stop the drive at named positions. For manual inspection set run to 0 and adjust phase. A full-speed loop ends at the same pose; looping at fractional run restarts at the phase setting.

An interpretation, not a replica

The cam and dimensions are Astra's original educational design. Return timing is prescribed; gravity, impact, wear and forging capacity are not simulated. The rounded tail is a fixed shoe. Small running gaps keep the rigid parts clear.

The Museum of Science explains the gravity trip hammer; the Venice exhibition describes its crank and intermittent stroke. No particular folio or invention priority is claimed.
