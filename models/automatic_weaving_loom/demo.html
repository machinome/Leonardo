The automatic loom

Vinci · Boldetti, 1954 · Codex Atlanticus, f.985r–v
Historical mechanism reconstruction

One drive. A connected loom.

The narrow-ribbon loom carries its spool between two hands. This is the Vinci museum interpretation, not a flying-shuttle loom. Orbit the actual CAD and use its stage buttons to inspect the transmission. The same shaft drives the captured head tracks and the gear train below.

Single engaging pin → six-position wheel → twelve-tooth wheel → scroll and descending rack → lower wheel pairs and perforated belt.
  1. Open shed · 60°
    Opposed captured followers separate the warp. The arm's rocking lever and cord control its approach.
  2. Handoff · 90°
    The hands pause together with both jaws closed. The donor then opens only after the receiving hand retains the spool.
  3. Beat right · 180°
    The spool clears the ribbon and the reed advances. Below, the intermittent train advances the winding apparatus.
  4. Return pick · 270°
    The shed and gripping sequence reverse. Both hands withdraw beyond the ribbon before the shed closes.

One-minute slow motion. Playback spans four input turns and eight picks in 60 seconds. At replay, the winding state resets; that reset is not an automatic reverse mechanism. Stage buttons stop the drive, and Run restores playback.

Follow the cords. Each external return weight rises when its driven vertical end descends. All six operating cords retain their length; the outboard springs close the receiving jaws.

Observed and inferred. The museum and manuscript establish the machine's arrangement. Concealed cam tracks, rockers, gripping details and fits are an explicitly documented mechanical completion, not measured museum geometry. Rope tension and spring closing are assumed; forces, wear and textile formation are not solved.
