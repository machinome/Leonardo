## Context

The pilot has commissioned all mechanisms as a historical reconstruction.
The historical reference is Leonardo's Atlanticus f.1078r; the construction
reference is MUST inventory IGB-37, designed by Arturo Uccelli and built by
Alessandro Siriati in 1952–1953. It is itself catalogued as an interpretative
model. Research inspected the original folio on e-Leo, all three photographs
on the requested page, and catalogue photographs dated 2009, 2015 and 2019.
`docs/reconstruction.md` records individual observations and URLs.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** reconstruct every visible functional system and its connections;
show the complete route by which wheel rotation saws and feeds; correct the
frame and channel; prove actual pin capture, blade retention, one-way feed,
rope attachment and winding; expose all mechanisms in the existing exhibit.

**Non-Goals:** fluid or cutting forces, wood fracture, structural capacity,
an assertion that every unseen fastener reproduces the museum object, or that
the later model is a surviving machine built by Leonardo. All geometric
dimensions not published by the museum are reconstruction dimensions.

## Decisions

1. Use X along feed, Y along the transverse wheel axle and Z up. The museum's
   approximate overall dimensions (785 × 505 × 645 mm) anchor presentation.
   A four-legged table carries rails and a two-upright saw tower; the wheel
   sits at the low channel, beside the crank and under the cutting plane.
2. Retain a crank/rod/slider with real bored eyes, retained pins, saw guides,
   iron blade clamps and a tension screw. Teeth face the incoming timber.
   The slotted log represents an already-started cut; no blade passes through
   intact wood merely to imitate cutting.
3. The long iron rocker and its short pawl arm are one rigid bell crank,
   pivoted on the table. An eyed tie joins its lower tip to the moving saw
   drive. The visible thin ties' exact seating is obscured in photographs;
   choose a mechanically closed connection at the saw's lower journal and
   label this interpretation, rather than leaving a disconnected lever.
4. Determine rocker angle by circle intersection at its tie, and driving
   pawl angle by the ratchet contact radius and its own fixed length. Set the
   short arm geometry so a complete stroke advances one tooth. The drum
   follows that contact on the working half; the holding pawl prevents reverse
   travel while the driving pawl recovers. Gravity/contact seating is a
   prescribed kinematic state, not a force solver. Test engagement separately
   from nominal coordinated motion.
5. Wind one continuous rope on the ratchet spindle, route it around the
   fixed end pulley and back to a retained carriage attachment. Wound length
   gained equals straight length lost. The selected groove/axial lane and
   termination are explicit choices where photographs conceal their routing.
6. Parameters remain a small, bounded set. The driver phase and timeline
   operate one physical input. All downstream freedom uses declared joints
   and relations; rope shape uses molejo. The four-turn presentation may
   reset only with an explicit replay label. Manual return requires releasing
   the pawls and unwinding; replay is not presented as a historical mechanism.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Perspective and obscured joints → record evidence confidence, retain the
  complete mechanism topology, verify kinematic closure and do not advertise
  a dimensionally exact museum replica.
- Pawl reset contact → densely sample tooth transitions, verify working and
  holding engagement, record any contact approximation quantitatively.
- Flexible rope is outside rigid integrity → dedicated path length, tangent,
  clearance and endpoint contracts, in addition to rigid tests.
- An old independently prescribed feed/readout can mask a defect → replace
  it with linked motion and derive exhibit measurements from export evidence.
- Geometry can pass while appearing unlike the source → inspect whole,
  shaft-axis and feed-detail snapshots beside the museum views.

## Migration Plan

Commit this plan, prove omissions red, replace the model in ground-up stages,
validate exact and browser behavior, update the public explanation and evidence,
sync specifications and archive. Preserve the earlier retrospective archive
as historical evidence of the simpler exhibit.

## Open Questions

The museum's undisclosed dimensions, rope termination and tiny tie fittings
are not recoverable exactly from these views. Their reconstruction choices
will be resolved through geometry and documented, not attributed to Leonardo.
