## Why

The pilot's 24 September 2026 commission is “goal: complete buildable frame
simulated.” The working hydraulic saw has a fused timber bed and tower:
it demonstrates motion but conceals overlapping members and cannot explain
how a carpenter would cut, fit and assemble the fixed structure.

## What Changes

- Replace the stationary bed, tower and their bearing supports with named,
  separate timber members and fitted wooden fasteners.
- Model the actual mortises, tenons, housings and peg holes; document timber
  stock, cutting operations, joint fits and erection order.
- Add an inspectable frame-only assembly study using the same parts as the
  working saw, with geometric insertion/retention and whole-root checks.
- Preserve the photographed layout and reliable moving transmission.
- Distinguish visible museum construction from inferred period-plausible
  joinery and dimensional choices; retain the museum-scale geometry.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `buildable-sawmill-frame`: cut timber inventory, fitted joints, assembly
  demonstration, historical evidence and construction limits.

### Modified Capabilities

None. Existing transmission, rectangular-frame and exhibition requirements
remain in force.

## Impact

Project-owned sawmill CAD, tests, exhibit links, construction documentation
and a frame-study export. No framework/viewer changes. The moving sash,
carriage, wheel and water channel remain later carpentry increments; this
commission completes the stationary supporting frame and its bearings.
The plinth remains an explicitly grounded display fixture. No claims of
full-size structural sizing, wood strength or historically measured joints.
