# Buildable sawmill frame Specification

## Purpose

Demonstrate the sawmill's fixed frame as buildable, inspectable period-plausible
carpentry while preserving its working mechanism and historical provenance.

## Requirements

### Requirement: Individually cut fixed timbers
The stationary frame SHALL consist of separately identifiable solid timber
members and fasteners with a stock and cutting schedule.

#### Scenario: Inspect any fixed frame member
- **WHEN** the visitor isolates a member
- **THEN** it is one connected wooden piece with its actual joint cuts,
  identifier, stock dimensions and cutting instructions available.

### Requirement: Fitted and retained carpentry
Every fixed timber connection SHALL have mating geometry and an identified
retention method compatible with period hand-tool carpentry.

#### Scenario: Fit and secure the joints
- **WHEN** the frame is assembled
- **THEN** its members do not interpenetrate, shoulders carry their mates,
  peg holes align, fasteners engage both mating parts, and the frame has a
  geometric load path to its named ground fixture.

### Requirement: Inspectable assembly order
The exhibit SHALL provide a frame study using the working machine's actual
parts and a documented feasible cutting and assembly sequence.

#### Scenario: Examine hidden joinery
- **WHEN** the visitor opens the carpentry study and separates its members
- **THEN** matching cuts and removable fasteners become visible and the
  documented assembly stages explain their order and insertion directions.

### Requirement: Preserve working mechanics and provenance
The joined frame SHALL support the existing transmission throughout its
complete demonstrated course and distinguish museum observations from
reconstruction assumptions.

#### Scenario: Run and compare the sawmill
- **WHEN** the saw completes its timber pass at supported kerf settings
- **THEN** its working clearances and motion remain valid, the photographed
  frame layout remains recognizable, and sources identify the limits of
  historical, dimensional and structural claims.
