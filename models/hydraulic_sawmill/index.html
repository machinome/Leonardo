About this reconstruction

The complete visible power path is represented: undershot wheel, retained crank and rod, two-post guided sash, pinned blade forks and tension nut, closed tie-and-rocker linkage, driving and holding pawls, 32-tooth ratchet and winding drum, continuous haul rope, fixed return pulley, and four-roller timber carriage.

The museum object is a modern interpretative model, not a surviving Leonardo machine. The photographs establish the arrangement; obscured tie seating, bearings, tooth profiles, fasteners and dimensions are explicit reconstruction choices. Read the illustration-by-illustration evidence and mechanism inventory or the museum’s detailed catalogue.

This is a geometric mechanism simulation, not a water, cutting or force solver. Pawls follow the tooth profiles with running clearance; gravity seating, taut rope and secured timber are assumed. The kerf is already open. The tension screw is shown set, without thread or blade-strain simulation. Each four-cycle loop visibly resets the demonstration: the historical operator would stop the water, release the pawls, unwind and reload the carriage, not automatically reverse it.
