Revolutions — Machinome

A wheel turns. A saw cuts. A carriage advances one small step.
Five centuries after Leonardo studied water-powered saws, a person directs
an AI tool to reconstruct one in source code.

Follow the waterwheel, crank, pulling pawl and two-rope feed, then see what
Machinome means: a machine's parts, connections and motion described in
code that people can inspect, change and test. Machine + genome.

The Milan object is an interpretative model from 1952–1953, designed by
Arturo Uccelli and built by Alessandro Siriati. This original digital
reconstruction is informed by that later interpretation and Leonardo's
study. It is not a surviving Renaissance machine or a measured historical
replica. Leonardo studied an existing tradition of hydraulic sawing.

The film shows selected, retimed motion from the model's 1460-second course.
The downstroke advances the carriage; the upstroke resets the driving hook
while the holding pawl retains the ratchet. Hidden layers expose mechanisms
for explanation. The visible kerf is a geometric illustration: fluid power,
cutting forces, wood fracture, rope tension and winding stability are not
simulated. No manufacturing or physical-performance validation is implied.

AI-production disclosure: the script, camera plan and production sources

The first-person voice and “ancestor” are literary devices connecting tools
