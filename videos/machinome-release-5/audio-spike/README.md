# Simulation-audio experiment

Status: experimental, not a framework API or a replacement release film.
Copyright (C) 2026 Luis Henrique Cassis Fagundes. AGPL-3.0-only.

The pilot authorized the opening spike after reviewing the layer design,
then approved extending it across the film with a listening-only player mixer.
The opening spike kept the production movie, narration, model, viewer and
Videomaker unchanged. Its audition captures the live stage and combines existing narration
with separately rendered water, wheel and saw tracks. No paid generation.

Boundaries:

- `capture.mjs`: film adapter; snapshots the resolved movie and captures its
  first 17 seconds, with immutable-source checks.
- `signals.py`: project adapter; evaluates the existing model's clock and
  existing kinematic functions, without building geometry or copying laws.
- `sawmill.json`: provisional sound recipe, no film timestamps.
- `synthesis.mjs`: model-independent, seeded, block-based audio synthesis.
- `build.mjs`: film time mapping, editorial reveal and narration mixing.
- `generate.mjs`: optional anonymous MMAudio asset authoring, not runtime.

Generated media, provider records, control tracks and verification go to a
new ignored `_build/sfx-spike-17-*` directory. Samples generated by MMAudio
are experimental inputs; this record grants no redistribution or commercial
rights. Preserve provider provenance and review rights before publication.

The authored acoustic response is illustrative. Flow, force, vibration,
historical sound and fluid dynamics are not simulated. Offline sound is the
first proof; live audition plays precomputed stems, not a real-time acoustic
simulation. The filmed model
is already moving at the first frame: acoustic introduction must not imply
that a newly solved hydraulic startup has been added.

## Completed audition

Output directory: `../_build/sfx-spike-17-DLBvT2/`.

- `opening-with-effects.mp4`: narration plus effects, 17.000 seconds,
  960x540, 24 fps, 408 frames, mono AAC at 48 kHz.
- `opening-effects-only.mp4`: same pictures, effects without narration.
- Separate `water.wav`, `paddles.wav`, `wheel.wav`, `saw.wav`, `effects.wav`
  and `mix.wav`; processed texture samples; model signals and event records.
- `manifest.json`, `audio-evidence.json`, captured frame PNGs and original
  source checksums retain provenance and numerical evidence.

The free MMAudio service returned `event: error / data: null` for both
text requests and the video-conditioned saw retry. No charge, login or
credential was involved; the three failed provider records remain in output.
The water reuses the previous successful generated opening. The saw instead
uses the public HQ preview of [Hand Saw Sawing Wood by deleted_user_7146007](https://freesound.org/people/deleted_user_7146007/sounds/383725/),
listed by its author on Freesound as CC0. Its source recording is kept as
`saw-recording.mp3` and its checksum in the manifest. The wheel uses seeded
procedural friction/resonances, not an obtained AI axle sample. The provider
fallback changes the sound asset, not the simulation-driven architecture.

The adapter evaluated 4,081 model poses on a 240 Hz control grid. The blade
position agrees with the checked-in CAD readouts to 0.000266 mm. Film time
uses Videomaker's actual resolved model-time mapping (about 0.786 model
seconds per film second). The saw's reveal runs from 7.208 to 8.408 seconds,
then its envelope follows blade speed/direction and timber engagement.
Twenty-six paddle crossings are scheduled at audio-sample resolution.
The first 17 seconds are constant-speed; rate changes are exercised by the
unit tests, not claimed as something visible in this clip.

Seven synthesis tests pass: independent water pitch, speed-dependent splash
cadence, speed-dependent saw pitch, stopped/disengaged behavior, exact seeded
replay across audio block sizes, no event backlog at a declared discontinuity,
and texture extraction. The actual 17-second four-stem render also reproduces
exactly with blocks of 128 and 4,096 samples. This is not yet a test of live
seek/restore, interactive latency or arbitrary running/clocked machines.

Final MP4s decode successfully. Mixed decoded audio measures -19.7 dBFS mean
and -0.7 dBFS peak. The mix keeps the narrator's timing and applies a constant
0.7593 final bus gain for headroom, plus effects-only ducking. The source
narration is unchanged. Original movie, stage, finishing script, narration
and full rehearsal checksums were rechecked after completion.

The live iframe capture includes a one-pixel CSS rounding row (960x541);
the first encode refused that odd height. Removing only the bottom row fixed
it; no frames were lost or recaptured. Mid-reveal and machine-visible images
were inspected. This is a live-preview capture: the player's known omitted
initial fade applies, while the approved machine reframe/fade is present.
No audio listening assessment was performed by the agent; the audition is
for the pilot's listening review, not a certified acoustic result.

## Reproduce

From the Leonardo repository, pass an unused output directory and the actual
Videomaker checkout; keep the existing live preview on port 8785:

```sh
node videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/capture.mjs OUTPUT VIDEOMAKER
```

Place the retained `saw-recording.mp3` in OUTPUT (or download the same public
preview URL in `manifest.json`). The water input is the retained earlier
`_build/sfx-audition-G66QJD/opening-effects.wav`. Then:

```sh
node --test videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/synthesis.test.mjs
node videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/build.mjs OUTPUT VIDEOMAKER
node videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/finish.mjs OUTPUT
```

The Python adapter uses the workspace venv currently named in `build.mjs`.
`generate.mjs OUTPUT saw|wheel [SILENT_VIDEO]` is an optional new free
provider request, never required for cached reproduction. The spike remains
uncommitted and project-local. Its opening-only implementation added no generic
soundtrack selector, framework declaration or viewer audio facility. The
subsequent full-film mixer is a separate Videomaker change, described below.

## Full-film listening build — 9 October 2026

The original spike above stays reproducible and unchanged. The full-length
extension adds `full-plan.mjs` (editorial sound spans and control interpolation),
`full-build.mjs` (audio-only build) and `full-verify.mjs` (PCM/timing checks).
`synthesis.mjs`, the recipe and the actual model adapter are unchanged.
This remains a project-owned proof of the future architecture, not a new
Machinome declaration or a physics claim.

Output: `../_build/sfx-full-cCdE5v/`. The four 24-bit mono, 48 kHz WAVs each
contain exactly 9,774,000 samples: **203.625 seconds**, on the film clock.
`stems.json` supplies them to Videomaker's experimental live mixer. Narration
is the existing `_build/narration.wav`, a separate channel; it is not copied,
regenerated, stretched, or replaced. No full-film MP4 is rendered by this build.

Editorial choices:

- The approved opening extends through the mechanical explanation. Effects
  fade out with the manuscript at 80.208–80.608 seconds.
- The model's code/browser demonstration fades sounds back at 88.458 seconds,
  at 65% of opening level, then fades them out during the final 0.3 seconds
  before the Studio rewind at 115.417 seconds.
- Scrubbing, discrete testing poses, source explanations and technology names
  have no mechanical sounds. This is an explicit film choice, not inferred
  physics or automatic visibility-based muting.
- Water and axle return with the physical wheel at 182.125–183.375 seconds.
  Paddle cadence follows its accelerating phase, without raising water pitch.
  No saw is heard here. Effects fade through the wheel hole at
  190.375–190.875 seconds; the closing logo/narration remains clear.

The first 17 seconds of **every individual stem are sample-identical** to the
approved spike, including its wheel/splash calibration. No whole-film RMS
normalization boosts the effects merely because later scenes are silent.
Full output repeats exactly with audio blocks of 128 and 4,096 samples.
The adapter evaluated 27,919 actual poses at 240 Hz; maximum disagreement
with the measured CAD blade readouts is 0.000266 mm. The ending produces 57
paddle crossings; successive spacing shortens from 0.545 to 0.0875 seconds.

Unlike the fixed 17-second audition mix, the mixer receives **unducked stems**,
so channels remain independent while listening. Starting levels are 55% for
each effect, 100% narration and 64.07% master. The actual default combined peak
is 0.8353; an absolute-sum bound of 0.88 leaves headroom for any mute/solo
combination at those defaults. Raising faders can still clip. Mixer choices
are temporary browser state and do not define a renderable mix.

Rebuild audio only, supplying a fresh output directory or omitting OUTPUT to
create one; the tool argument is the checkout used for film-time mapping:

```sh
node --test videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/synthesis.test.mjs videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/full-plan.test.mjs
node videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/full-build.mjs VIDEOMAKER [OUTPUT]
node videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/full-verify.mjs OUTPUT
```

The 12 pure tests and generated-audio verification passed. Verification pins
silent spans, exact opening preservation, fixed water pitch, accelerated
cadence and unchanged hashes of movie/narration/stage/finisher sources,
the evaluated movie, narration WAV and old rehearsal MP4. Per-source hashes,
recipe, signals, events and default mix measurements are retained in output.
Browser mixer checks are a separate proof, not an acoustic listening review.
The original generated water's publication-rights caveat remains in force.

The final live preview is http://127.0.0.1:8785/#movie, served by Videomaker's
isolated `live-audio-mixer` branch, implementation `866c25a`. The feature is
committed there, not integrated or pushed. `inspect-mixer.mjs` passes 17
real-film checks against that final server, using native audio scheduling
and analyser output: five synchronized channels, each effect soloed,
all-muted clock progression, rapid seeks, silent pause, stable geometry
across 12 scene positions and two-second arrow shortcuts. The machine and
ending screenshots were visually inspected. Recheck the running player with:

```sh
node videos/machinome-release-5/audio-spike/inspect-mixer.mjs VIDEOMAKER http://127.0.0.1:8785 videos/machinome-release-5/_build/sfx-full-cCdE5v
```

Results and screenshots overwrite only those generated verification files;
no video is rendered. The mixer settings remain temporary and are not saved.

## Previous revision: stretched hacksaw and iron axle/crank — 9 October 2026

The pilot rejected the preceding saw's motorized character and the wooden
axle sound. Current output is `../_build/sfx-mechanics-jgZGkp/`, served at the
same player address. The earlier outputs are retained, not overwritten.

- **Hacksaw:** [Handheld Hacksaw.wav by aidansamuel](https://freesound.org/people/aidansamuel/sounds/540143/),
  explicitly recorded cutting plywood, CC0. Four complete scraping strokes
  replace the old grain-flattened sound. Input segments are pitch-preserving
  stretched to about two seconds, then read according to actual blade travel.
  This gives faster/higher-pitched cutting when the animation accelerates,
  quiet direction changes and a return stroke at 10% gain. Source microtexture
  is retained rather than rebuilt as a constant motor-like loop.
- **Iron axle / crank:** [Metal Squeak 2.wav by rdneubauer](https://freesound.org/people/rdneubauer/sounds/93783/),
  a metal fireplace door creaking, CC0. Short filtered joint-friction sounds
  replace the old synthetic wooden resonances. Events follow shaft phase;
  speed changes cadence, with bounded pitch shift and short fades. This is a
  timbral proxy for the pilot's iron mechanism, not a field recording of it.

Only those two full-length WAVs change. Water/paddles are copied byte-for-byte,
narration/pictures stay unchanged, and every default mixer gain is preserved.
The default PCM peak is 0.8022, absolute-sum bound 0.8575; no extra attenuation
was required. The 53 iron-friction events, source hashes, recipe and model
signal key are retained. The original 17-second equality still applies to
water/paddles; the pilot explicitly requested changes to the other two sounds.

The new anonymous MMAudio hacksaw request failed with a null provider error;
its prompt and receipt are preserved. No fresh AI-generated saw/axle is
claimed. `generate.mjs` now includes an explicit manual-hacksaw prompt and
an iron-axle/crank/levers prompt instead of the old wooden-axle direction.

`mechanics.mjs` is pure sample synthesis; `mechanics.json` owns source
segments, timbre and provisional artistic settings; `revise-mechanics.mjs`
reuses validated real-model coordinates and refuses a changed model/adapter
or movie. No new CAD evaluation or tool/framework interface is involved.
Eighteen tests and the completed-output verifier pass, including exact
block replay and stopping/discontinuity behavior. The extraction-length
guard also exposed and corrected a pre-preview clipping error; rejected
`sfx-hacksaw-gIikor` was never served and remains marked as rejected.


## Current revision: original-pitch manual scrapes — 9 October 2026

The pilot rejected the preceding hacksaw as still electrically powered in
character. The current output is `../_build/sfx-manual-saw-9gNoO1/`, channel
**Manual saw**. It removes the fivefold time stretch and displacement-based
sample scan entirely. Only original 0.40–0.44s hand-tool scrapes are played,
one per eligible cutting/return stroke, at playback rate 1. Decode to mono,
contiguous trimming, constant gain and 8ms edge fades are the only source
processing. There is no filtering, grain loop, repeated filler or oscillator.

`manual-saw.mjs` schedules from the actual blade-position/velocity controls;
`manual-saw.json` records source trims and the simple human-action prompt.
Its timing follows the blade but pitch does not follow velocity in this
audition. The 35 short scrapes occupy 14.304s of the film, with silence
between them instead of an extended friction bed. This is an explicit
manual-foley trial, not a physical tooth-contact sound model. The previous
pitch-responsive experiments remain reproducible in their original sources.

The prompt sent was exactly: “A person slowly sawing a wooden board by hand,
with separate push-and-pull scrapes and quiet gaps.” No machine/power-tool
description was sent in either prompt. The anonymous service again returned
a null error; the retained CC0 hand-saw recording, not generated audio,
supplies the playback. Prompt and response evidence remain in the output.

All three other stems are copied byte-for-byte from the preceding revision,
including the iron axle/crank. Narration, visuals and every initial mixer
gain remain unchanged. Mixed peak is 0.7877; absolute headroom bound 0.8205.
Twenty-four unit tests and completed-output verification pass, including
