# Cut and Advance

Leonardo's hydraulic sawmill does two jobs with one waterwheel: it drives
the saw's stroke and feeds the timber forward one ratchet tooth per stroke.
This short film follows the drive train, reads the line of source that
couples wheel to crankshaft while the picture highlights the same parts,
watches the two pawls take turns in slow motion, follows the two ropes to
the carriage and closes on the rhythm of cut and advance.

The model presents prescribed kinematics: slow motion is a playback choice,
not physics; forces, cutting loads and material removal are not simulated.
The geometry is an educational reconstruction informed by Leonardo's drawing
and the later Milan museum interpretation, not a measured Renaissance
artifact.
