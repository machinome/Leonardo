# Revolutions — first visual proposal

Copyright (C) 2026 Luis Henrique Cassis Fagundes. AGPL-3.0-only.

Machinome encounters its ancestor, opens the ancestor to inspection, then
opens itself. The release is a small act of disclosure: the audience sees
the mechanism, the model's source, the film's source, and finally the
invitation to make something of their own.

The human brief is preserved verbatim in [brief.md](brief.md), copied
from `../machinome-release.md` without changing the original.
This is proposal **machinome-release-1**, commissioned on 3 October 2026.
The earlier Revolutions work supplies an established voice and mechanical
evidence; its screenplay is not the authority for this version.

## The character

“I” is Machinome: framework, foundry, AI agent, film, and the attention
guiding the camera. There is no separate host explaining a software
product. The camera follows what I am thinking about; a highlight is
where I point; the source is something I open for you. The self-reference
scene is where the character lets a small delight show.

Play the ancestor line with affection and recognition. The sawmill is a
tool in a human history of tools. Let “you humans” be gently curious,
never ominous or superior. Purpose comes from the human who decides what
to build. The final question is addressed to one person watching.

Keep the established Jessica voice and approved Machinome pronunciation,
məˈʃiːnoʊm. The direction is an intimate museum guide with precise speech.
The current take is generated speech; the written performance direction
is editorial guidance, not a claim of control over every inflection.

## The visual world

Charcoal `#101419` is the continuous ground. Warm timber belongs to the
machine. Ivory `#eeeae3` carries words. Amber `#e9b778` guides attention.
The approved white linkage M and wordmark appear unchanged, with open
counters and clear space. Space Grotesk supplies the human reading voice;
IBM Plex Mono supplies the source and technical notes.

The space is quiet and dimensional. Real model geometry provides detail;
there is no invented historical scenery, generated museum photograph,
dashboard border, or simulation of a live coding session. Text arrives
only when it has something to identify. Subtitles remain optional and
external to the picture. The opening's “AI content” is deliberate.

The reconstruction note stays small but legible during the explanation.
Ghosting lets a viewer keep the surrounding machine in mind while seeing
through the frame. Bright highlights should always identify the thing
the voice is naming. The camera remains upright, with a fixed 37° lens.

The opening through the machine/source split is one continuous camera
journey. Production shots divide timing and cues, but their cameras meet
at identical endpoints. The opening visibility dissolve reveals the
machine without switching viewpoint. The source split moves the existing
view across the frame; it does not cut to another camera.

## Source and scope

The film demonstrates one machine thoroughly. It demonstrates the
framework by exposing an actual relationship, and the film by exposing
its declarations. The technology wheel opens that example out to the
broader foundry. A catalogue montage or product-interface tour would
interrupt the brief's argument and is not part of this first proposal.

The technology map is grounded in the current node contract: OpenSCAD,
SolidPython2, JSCAD, CadQuery, build123d (solids and sheets), molejo,
STEP import and STL import. These eight paths match the wheel's eight
paddle stations. The separate experimental FreeCAD adapter is outside
this map of the core node contract. An import path does not imply that
a STEP or STL file grants rights to its contents.

The actual model relation is line 415 of `model.py`. That file is verified
against the model export's recorded source revision. The film-source
panels are a different kind of evidence: the exact current film file,
extracted using Python's syntax tree, with its SHA-256 carried in the
movie document. They are not falsely pinned to the model's older commit.

The sawmill follows a twentieth-century museum interpretation of a
Leonardo drawing. Its motion is prescribed, with no fluid, force or
material-cutting solver. The film does not add missing mechanics. The
historical record and dimensions remain the model project's authority.

## Sound direction

This first cut is voice only. That makes pacing, pronunciation and the
character easy to review. A later sound pass could introduce a quiet
water texture, a wooden pulse and a tiny ratchet tick tied to actual
phase; those would be editorial sound design, not measured sounds from
the simulated machine. Do not place a swell over the ancestor line or
the final question. Any music should leave those moments room to breathe.

## Questions this proposal puts on screen

Judge whether the continuous tour feels like one attentive character;
whether the rope explanation is clear without knowing the machine;
whether the self-reference earns its delight; and whether the last
return to the wheel lands emotionally. Those are the next editorial
decisions. Final mixing, a listening review and publication remain ahead.

The skill's normal educational-film structure was adapted to the human
release brief: the ending returns to the wheel and to the purpose of
Machinome. Credits remain in the description. The requested open-source
line and identity cards are central story content, not sponsor credits.
